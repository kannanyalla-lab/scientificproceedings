--- abstract
Intraoperative ultrasound (IOUS) is a real-time imaging modality with considerable potential to improve the safety and outcomes of neurosurgical procedures. It provides real-time image guidance and is radiation-free, repeatable, readily available, easily integrated into the existing operating room workflow, and more cost-effective than other intraoperative imaging modalities such as intraoperative magnetic resonance imaging (iMRI) and intraoperative computed tomography (iCT). The clinical applications of IOUS are broad and include brain and spinal tumors, intracranial hematomas, stereotactic biopsies, and catheter guidance. In this article, we briefly describe the various applications of IOUS and share our experience with its use at our center.

## Introduction
Intraoperative imaging technologies continue to evolve and have become integral to modern neurosurgical practice. Reliable intraoperative orientation is one of the most important determinants of safe and effective neurosurgery.^1^ Image-guided modalities such as frame-based and frameless neuronavigation, intraoperative ultrasound (IOUS), intraoperative computed tomography (iCT), and intraoperative magnetic resonance imaging (iMRI) enable neurosurgeons to accurately localize lesions and adjacent structures, facilitate surgical planning, and achieve safe maximal resection.

Neuronavigation is affected by brain shift that occurs once surgery begins, mainly due to cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) drainage, and therefore does not provide real-time navigation.^2^ The other three modalities—IOUS, intraoperative CT (iCT), and intraoperative MRI (iMRI)—provide real-time imaging and accurate information during surgery.^3^ Although intraoperative CT and MRI are more advanced imaging modalities, they are expensive and require infrastructure and logistics that are not feasible in most operating room settings.

IOUS, on the other hand, can be easily integrated into the existing operating room workflow. Recent advances in technology have significantly improved the image quality of IOUS.

In this article, we share how IOUS helps in various neurosurgical procedures and describe our experience with its use at our center.

## Gadgets
We initially used Sonosite probes, which had large footprints. Over the last year, we acquired the BK5000 neurosurgical transducers, which are specialized, high-resolution, sterilizable transducers designed specifically for neurosurgical applications. Disposable, easy-to-use needle guides assist in accurate targeting of lesions.

## Conclusion
IOUS clearly defines tumor margins and facilitates safe tumor resection. It improves the extent of resection of intraparenchymal brain tumors. Furthermore, early tumor-bed hematomas can be recognized, as they have a characteristic appearance on ultrasound. It does not require any changes in patient positioning or the intraoperative setting, while at the same time offering excellent spatial and temporal resolution. Real-time intraoperative imaging technologies have shown utility in assisting tumor resection in a dynamic surgical field.^5^ Increasing attention has been paid to IOUS, which has been reported to be a widely accessible, portable, space-saving, and reliable imaging modality that enables surgeons to follow and modify the surgical plan in real time.
