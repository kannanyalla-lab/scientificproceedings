--- abstract
Statin intolerance due to muscle-related symptoms is a common cause of poor adherence to lipid-lowering therapy. Bempedoic acid and ezetimibe are frequently used as second-line alternatives in such patients. Although uncommon, muscle-related adverse events may occur with bempedoic acid. We report the case of a middle-aged man with a history of statin intolerance who developed severe myalgias following initiation of bempedoic acid therapy. Symptoms resolved promptly following drug discontinuation. We propose that despite its rarity, a high index of suspicion may help identify such adverse events, especially in individuals with previously documented statin-related muscle symptoms.

## Introduction
Statin-associated muscle symptoms (SAMS) are well-described and may include myalgia, muscle tenderness, or, in severe cases, rhabdomyolysis.^1^ Patients with statin intolerance may need alternative lipid-lowering therapies for primary and secondary prevention of cardiovascular disease. A combination of bempedoic acid and ezetimibe is recommended in such patients. Ezetimibe is not associated with muscle toxicity. However, bempedoic acid has rarely been implicated in muscle-related adverse events.^2^ We report the case of a middle-aged man with a prior history of SAMS who developed muscle symptoms following initiation of bempedoic acid, highlighting the need for a high index of suspicion, as this uncommon adverse effect may be overlooked as a cause of muscle symptoms.

## Case report
A 40-year-old man presented to the outpatient department with an insidious onset of progressively worsening pain in both lower limbs for the past three weeks. He described the pain as dull and aching, mostly involving both thighs, buttocks, and calves. The aches aggravated on standing for a while; however, assuming the supine position did not bring significant relief. There was no radiation of pain, and there was no associated weakness, paresthesia, or numbness. Over the preceding few days, these aches had become disabling enough to prevent him from working out at the gym or even walking.

He was neither diabetic nor hypertensive. Routine blood investigations performed a year earlier had revealed elevated total and LDL cholesterol levels, for which he had been prescribed rosuvastatin 5 mg once daily for primary prevention in view of a family history of coronary artery disease. However, within a few days of starting therapy, he developed muscle aches that were much more severe than those experienced during the present episode. Evaluation at that time revealed elevated creatine kinase (CK) levels, without evidence of rhabdomyolysis. Rosuvastatin was discontinued, and the symptoms resolved promptly, supporting a diagnosis of statin-associated muscle symptoms (SAMS).

On examination during the recent episode, he was found well-built and nourished. General examination and systemic examinations were unremarkable, and there was no muscle tenderness. On further inquiry, he revealed that he had recently been started on bempedoic acid 180 mg and ezetimibe 10 mg once daily following a repeat lipid profile that showed a total cholesterol level of 203 mg/dL and an LDL cholesterol level of 160 mg/dL. Given his previous history of SAMS, the treating physician had prescribed this combination and reassured him that these agents are generally not associated with muscle symptoms. Laboratory investigations, including CK levels, were within normal limits. However, the total cholesterol and LDL cholesterol levels had decreased to 140 mg/dL and 74 mg/dL respectively. Other tests, such as vitamin D and TSH, were within normal limits. After discussion with the patient, the combination lipid-lowering therapy was stopped. Five days after cessation, he reported marked improvement in his symptoms, and by the end of one week, he was completely asymptomatic. He was reassured and advised to repeat the lipid profile after a month to determine the need for and options for further lipid-lowering therapy.

## Discussion
Statin-associated muscle symptoms (SAMS) include various manifestations such as muscle aches, soreness, cramps, weakness, and, in extreme cases, rhabdomyolysis.^3^ With an estimated prevalence of 9%, SAMS is considered a major reason for statin intolerance or non-adherence, leading to increased cardiovascular risk.^4^ Major risk factors for SAMS include older age, low body mass index, previous statin intolerance, hypothyroidism, vitamin D deficiency, higher doses of statins, strenuous exercise, dehydration, and interactions with certain drugs like fibrates.^1^

For patients with SAMS, several alternative lipid-lowering approaches have been recommended. These include using lower statin doses, switching to hydrophilic statins such as rosuvastatin or pravastatin, or substituting non-statin agents such as ezetimibe and bempedoic acid.^1,5,6^ In patients who are unable to tolerate these therapies or fail to achieve target lipid levels, PCSK9 inhibitors such as alirocumab and evolocumab may be considered. However, these agents are injectable and relatively expensive.^3,7^

This case highlights a rare but clinically significant adverse effect of bempedoic acid in a patient with previously documented SAMS. Bempedoic acid is generally considered safe in statin-intolerant individuals as it is activated predominantly in the liver and has minimal activity in skeletal muscle.^6^ As a result, muscle-related side effects are considered uncommon. However, our patient developed disabling myalgias within 2-3 weeks of initiation of bempedoic acid–ezetimibe combination therapy, with complete resolution of symptoms following drug withdrawal. This temporal relationship suggests a possible causal association.

An additional noteworthy feature in this case was the patient's normal creatine kinase (CK) level despite marked muscle symptoms. This observation underscores that muscle symptoms potentially related to bempedoic acid may occur despite normal creatine kinase levels and that a drug-related etiology should not be excluded solely on the basis of normal laboratory parameters. The prior history of SAMS may also indicate an underlying susceptibility to muscle-related adverse effects from lipid-lowering therapies.^6^

With increasing use of bempedoic acid in persons with SAMS, clinicians should remain aware of the possibility of muscle-related adverse effects, particularly in patients with a previous history of SAMS. A high index of suspicion, early recognition, and withdrawal of the drug may prevent unnecessary investigations and improve patient quality of life.

## Conclusion
Although bempedoic acid is generally considered a safe and effective alternative in statin-intolerant patients, rare muscle-related adverse effects may occur. This case highlights the importance of maintaining a high index of suspicion for drug-related myalgias, even in the presence of normal creatine kinase levels, particularly in individuals with previously documented statin-associated muscle symptoms. Early recognition and withdrawal of the offending agent may prevent unnecessary investigations and improve patient outcomes.
